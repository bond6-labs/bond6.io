# Bond6 Event Cross-Currents

## Purpose

An Event Cross-Current is a live, evidence-led Bonding route for a single named event where human work and AI are materially connected. It is not a verdict on the event, a popularity chart, or a substitute for specialist verification.

It complements the normal Bonding route:

- **Normal route:** one Human Pulse story and one AI Wave story share a visible editorial bridge.
- **Event Cross-Current:** two or more independent reports cover the same named event and make both the human and AI dimensions explicit.

## Admission rule

An event must meet every condition below during the rolling seven-day window:

1. Each included story contains a named mathematical question, theorem, conjecture, hypothesis, or problem.
2. Each included story also contains a concrete AI, formal-verification, or proof signal.
3. At least two distinct tracked publishers report the same normalized event name.
4. The cluster, taken together, contains both an AI signal and a human-contribution signal.
5. Bond6 shows at most three distinct-source readings, ordered by discovery time.

Known language-specific names are retained for clarity; a constrained named-phrase extractor supports future English mathematical events without a one-off code path. A story that merely mentions AI, a theorem, or a famous mathematician does not qualify.

## Claim status

If source text contains claim, proposal, draft, verification, review, or scrutiny language, the route must display **“Claims under independent review” / “证明主张仍待独立核验.”** It must not state or imply that a theorem has been settled.

The route links only to the original reports and preserves their author and publisher attribution. It does not create a generated Bonding edition.

## Focusing

The same event can surface in Focusing with its named title, independent-source count, and claim status. This is separate from the broader **Mathematics, proof & AI / 数学证明与 AI** subject signal. Two sources are sufficient for an explicitly labelled emerging event; three or more become strong. Single-source announcements never become an Event Cross-Current.

## Example: Riemann hypothesis claims

If two independent reports describe both an AI-assisted Riemann proof claim and a human proof draft, the site may show a Riemann hypothesis Event Cross-Current. Its status remains “under independent review” until reliable source language supports a different status.

## Guardrails and review

- RSS availability is not proof of a claim. A missing article from one feed is never silently replaced by an assertion of fact.
- Official lab or university announcements can be useful primary context, but do not alone satisfy the independent-reporting rule.
- Event grouping is language-specific: English and Simplified Chinese originals remain in their own editions.
- The route expires naturally after seven days unless fresh qualifying reporting arrives.
- New event families should be added with fixture tests covering qualification, single-source rejection, and claim-status wording.
